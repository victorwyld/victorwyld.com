October 24, 2026

The Ownership Answer

What It Is Like

Victor Wyld has published The Ownership Answer: What It Is Like, the fifth paper in his 2026 philosophical sequence and a direct engagement with David Chalmers's Hard Problem of consciousness.

The paper challenges the inference that the explanatory gap between physical processes and subjective experience establishes the need for further fundamental phenomenal properties or psychophysical laws.

Its central proposal is Ownership: the immediate, objective relation by which an occurrence belongs to whatever is implicated in it. Ownership precedes awareness, recognition, and consciousness.

From that starting point, the paper advances a positive account of sensation and consciousness. Hurting is identified with the sensory occurrence itself, while consciousness involves awareness under scrutiny and what the participant must carry.

The paper's critical argument examines what Chalmers has established. Its positive identity thesis is offered as an inference to the best explanation, rather than as a deductive proof.

The Hard Problem keeps its observation and loses its conclusion.

Published October 24, 2026, the paper is available through Zenodo and PhilPapers, with an accompanying introduction on this website.

The Acquisition Problem

Participational Recognition and the Structural Acquisition of Implication

The fourth paper distinguishes representational recognition from participational recognition and examines how recognition may acquire implication for a participant's continuation.

It develops the distinction between automatic and contested incorporation, examining how recognition may leave a remainder that must be carried.

The Participanthood Problem

Identity Under Continued Carrying

The third paper examines what becomes of a participant when implication continues across time and contributes to identity.

It identifies five modes of identity transformation: accumulation, reorientation, revision, fracture, and integration.

May 29, 2026

The Recompression Problem

The Hard Problem, Scrutinized Awareness, and the Institutional Life of Difficulty

The second paper develops the problem of recompression: the process by which distinctions established through philosophical analysis can later become functionally interchangeable through labels, proxies, classifications, or institutional practices.

It introduces compression resistance and the collapse map as ways of identifying where an operative distinction may be lost.

May 22, 2026

On the Question of Scrutinized Awareness

An Inquiry into Recognition, Participation, and Interiority

The foundational paper introducing Scrutinized Awareness Theory (SAT), a positive threshold theory of consciousness.

SAT proposes that awareness becomes consciousness when it becomes something the participant must carry. The framework distinguishes consciousness from awareness, intelligence, responsiveness, memory, and symbolic performance.
