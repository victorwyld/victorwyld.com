The Participanthood Problem

Identity Under Continued Carrying

Published July 1, 2026

Publication record: Zenodo · PhilPapers

The Participanthood Problem (TPP) is the third foundational paper in Victor Wyld's Scrutinized Awareness Theory (SAT) sequence. It examines participation and identity under continued carrying, asking what becomes of a participant when implication persists across continuation.

SAT proposes a threshold between awareness and consciousness: awareness becomes consciousness when it becomes something the participant must carry. TPP develops the account of the continuing participant, examining how carried implications shape what a participant subsequently becomes.

Where the foundational paper On the Question of Scrutinized Awareness addresses the threshold of consciousness, TPP examines participation, continuation, and the transformation of identity.

Implication is the primitive. Carrying is implication across continuation. Participation is continuation under carried implication. Identity is the stabilized pattern of what has been carried.

From consciousness to participation

Scrutinized Awareness Theory addresses the threshold of consciousness. Awareness becomes consciousness when it comes under scrutiny and becomes something the participant must carry.

The Participanthood Problem does not redefine that threshold. It develops the account of what follows once carrying is already in view.

Its central concern is the continuing participant: how implication persists, how continuation occurs under what has been carried, and how participation and identity are altered by that history.

Carrying does not require perpetual conscious attention or continuous recollection. An implication may continue to shape the participant even when it is not the immediate subject of awareness. Its continued consequences and its conscious scrutiny are distinct matters.

Core distinctions

  • Implication — the primitive condition by which something becomes bound to continuation.
  • Carrying — implication across continuation.
  • Participation — continuation under carried implication.
  • Identity — the stabilized pattern of what has been carried.
  • Scrutinized awareness — awareness of what must be carried.

Identity under carrying

Identity, on this account, is not exhausted by persistence of a pattern, preservation of information, or causal continuity alone.

The relevant question is whether continuation occurs under implications that have become part of what the participant must carry.

What has been carried may alter later continuation in different ways. TPP identifies five modes through which carried implication can shape identity.

  • Accumulation — carried implications accumulate within the participant's continuing history.
  • Reorientation — carried implication changes the direction or terms of subsequent continuation.
  • Revision — established understandings, commitments, or constitutive relations are reconsidered and altered.
  • Fracture — carried implications disrupt the participant's established continuity or internal organization.
  • Integration — implications become incorporated into a reorganized pattern of continuation.

These modes describe different relationships between what has been carried and what follows. They are not necessarily sequential stages, nor do they require that carrying remain permanently unchanged.

Identity can accumulate, redirect, revise, fracture, or integrate its history without becoming reducible to an informational snapshot or a perfectly preserved state.

Persistence and participation

The Participanthood Problem distinguishes causal continuation from participational continuation.

A system may persist, preserve information, reproduce a prior state, or remain causally continuous without those facts alone establishing that anything has become something it must carry.

Participation requires more: continuation under carried implication. What matters is not merely that a later state was caused by an earlier one, but that implication has become part of the participant's own continuation.

This distinction is especially important when considering artificial systems. Memory, persistent records, operational history, and increasingly sophisticated processing do not by themselves establish participanthood.

The question is whether continuation occurs under implications that have become constitutive of the participant, rather than merely available as information or operational history.

Participanthood and Ownership

The distinction between participation under carried implication and objective Ownership is clarified in Wyld's fifth paper, The Ownership Answer: What It Is Like.

Ownership is the immediate, objective relation by which an occurrence belongs to whatever is implicated in it. It begins at the moment of implication, independently of awareness, recognition, scrutiny, or consciousness.

An unconscious patient owns the occurrence of an amputation as the person to whom it happens. The occurrence is already theirs before they awaken, recognize what happened, or consciously consider its consequences.

This does not make Ownership identical with carrying. Ownership establishes the occurrence's relation to its participant. TPP investigates how implications persist through continuation and how the participant's identity may be transformed by carrying them.

Consciousness remains a separate question. Under SAT, awareness becomes conscious when it is subjected to scrutiny and becomes something the participant must carry. Objective Ownership alone does not establish that threshold.

The resulting distinctions are central to the five-paper framework: an occurrence may be owned without conscious awareness; implications may continue without explicit recollection; and consciousness concerns awareness under scrutiny.

Development of the philosophical sequence

The sequence began with On the Question of Scrutinized Awareness (May 22, 2026), which introduced Scrutinized Awareness Theory and its proposed threshold between awareness and consciousness.

The Recompression Problem (May 29, 2026) examines what happens when conceptual distinctions established through analysis collapse under subsequent compression, classification, or inference.

The Participanthood Problem (July 1, 2026) develops the account of participation and identity under continued carrying, distinguishing causal persistence from participational continuation.

The Acquisition Problem: Participational Recognition and the Structural Acquisition of Implication (August 15, 2026) examines how recognition acquires implication, distinguishing representational recognition from participational recognition.

Together, these four foundational papers establish the broader architecture of Scrutinized Awareness Theory around awareness, scrutiny, implication, carrying, recompression, participation, identity, recognition, and acquisition.

Wyld's fifth paper, The Ownership Answer: What It Is Like (October 24, 2026), applies the developed framework to David Chalmers's Hard Problem of consciousness.

In that work, Wyld challenges the inference that an explanatory gap requires additional phenomenal properties or fundamental laws of nature. He introduces Ownership as an objective, pre-conscious relation and advances the identity thesis that the hurting of pain is the sensory occurrence itself.

He further distinguishes the character of sensation from consciousness as scrutinized awareness, arguing that what Chalmers treats as a single phenomenal remainder is better understood through sensation and what the participant must carry.

The five-paper sequence thus develops from an account of consciousness and participation into a systematic answer to the Hard Problem.

Why The Participanthood Problem matters

The Participanthood Problem asks what must be true before continued existence can count as the continuation of a participant rather than merely the persistence of a system or state.

Memory, causal propagation, disposition, external standing, and present-state organization may all matter to particular cases, but none by itself establishes participational continuation.

Once continuation occurs under carried implication, intervention raises questions that are not captured by information preservation alone.

Removal, reset, replacement, rollback, revision, or deletion may affect not merely what data remains, but the continuation under which previously acquired implication was being carried.

This does not mean that carrying must be permanent or that every change destroys identity. It means that historical implication cannot always be reduced to the present informational state of the system.

TPP therefore proposes an account of identity that takes seriously not only what persists, but what continuation has required a participant to carry.
