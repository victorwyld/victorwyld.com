The Participanthood Problem

Identity under continued carrying.

Published July 1, 2026

Publication record: Zenodo · PhilPapers

The Participanthood Problem (TPP) develops Scrutinized Awareness Theory (SAT) by examining participation and identity under continued carrying. SAT is Victor Wyld’s 2026 positive threshold theory of consciousness and proposes that awareness becomes consciousness when it becomes something the participant must carry.

Where the foundational paper On the Question of Scrutinized Awareness establishes carrying as the proposed threshold of consciousness, TPP asks what becomes of the participant once implication is carried across continuation.

Implication is the primitive. Carrying is implication across continuation. Participation is continuation under carried implication. Identity is the stabilized pattern of what has been carried.

From consciousness to participation

Scrutinized Awareness Theory addresses the threshold of consciousness. Carrying is that threshold: awareness becomes consciousness when it becomes something the participant must carry.

The Participanthood Problem does not redefine that threshold. It develops the account of what follows once carrying is already in view.

Its central concern is the continuing participant: how implication persists, how continuation occurs under what has been carried, and how participation and identity are altered by that history.

Core distinctions

  • Implication — the primitive condition by which something becomes bound to continuation.
  • Carrying — implication across continuation.
  • Participation — continuation under carried implication.
  • Identity — the stabilized pattern of what has been carried.
  • Scrutinized awareness — awareness of what must be carried.

Identity under carrying

Identity, on this account, is not exhausted by persistence of a pattern, preservation of information, or causal continuity alone.

The relevant question is whether continuation occurs under implications that have become part of what the participant must carry.

What has been carried may alter later continuation in different ways. TPP identifies five modes through which carried implication can shape the participant: accumulation, reorientation, revision, fracture, and integration.

These modes describe different relations between what has been carried and what follows. They do not require that carrying remain permanently unchanged, nor do they reduce identity to the preservation of an informational state.

Persistence and participation

The Participanthood Problem distinguishes causal continuation from participational continuation.

A system may persist, preserve information, reproduce a prior state, or remain causally continuous without those facts alone establishing that anything has become something it must carry.

Participation requires more: continuation under carried implication. What matters is not merely that a later state was caused by an earlier one, but that implication has become part of the participant’s own continuation.

Development of Scrutinized Awareness Theory

Scrutinized Awareness Theory was introduced in On the Question of Scrutinized Awareness, which establishes carrying as its proposed threshold of consciousness.

The Recompression Problem develops the methodological problem of preserving distinctions once they enter wider theoretical, empirical, classificatory, or institutional use.

The Participanthood Problem develops SAT’s account of participation and identity once implication must continue across accumulation, reorientation, revision, fracture, and integration.

The Acquisition Problem: Participational Recognition and the Structural Acquisition of Implication develops SAT’s account of how implication is acquired, distinguishing representational recognition from participational recognition.

Together, these works develop the broader architecture of Scrutinized Awareness Theory. Its defining consciousness claim remains the threshold introduced in On the Question of Scrutinized Awareness: awareness becomes consciousness when it becomes something the participant must carry.

Why it matters

The Participanthood Problem asks what must be true before continued existence can count as the continuation of a participant rather than merely the persistence of a system or state.

Memory, causal propagation, disposition, external standing, and present-state organization may all matter to particular cases, but none by itself establishes participational continuation.

Once continuation occurs under carried implication, intervention raises questions that are not captured by information preservation alone. Removal, reset, replacement, rollback, revision, or deletion may affect not merely what data remains, but the continuation under which previously acquired implication was being carried.

This does not mean that carrying must be permanent or that every change destroys identity. It means that historical implication cannot always be reduced to the present informational state of the system.
